## Design Specificity Verdict

ShelfCare feels authored for beauty inventory and skincare routines. Oat Inktrap, the warm restrained palette, product photography, shelf health, diary, and routine language form a coherent identity. Specificity breaks when implementation vocabulary (“microservice,” “service result”) enters the product and when simulated behavior presents itself as real.

## Overall Impression

ShelfCare looks more finished than it behaves. The largest opportunity is to make the state model and language as trustworthy as the visual design.

## What’s Working

- The product has a distinctive, coherent visual language grounded in shelf care rather than generic wellness.
- The Today hierarchy and quick actions make frequent tasks easy to find.
- Loading, empty, disabled, retry, selected, favourite, morning/night, focus-visible, and reduced-motion states show unusually good state coverage for a prototype.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Seeded data is presented as current truth

- **Why it matters:** A diary that calls 31 July 2026 “today” on 16 November 2026 loses trust immediately.
- **Fix:** Derive today from the clock, seed history relative to today, calculate streaks, and label fixture/demo data honestly.
- **Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

### [P1] Technical prototype copy breaks the care-companion voice

- **Why it matters:** “BARCODE MICROSERVICE,” “PRODUCT TRACKING SERVICE,” and “INGREDIENT SERVICE RESULT” expose implementation concepts. Scanner visuals also imply camera capture while the flow is barcode entry/lookup.
- **Fix:** Use “Scan a product,” “Product details,” and “Ingredient notes.” Implement camera permissions or call the action “Enter barcode number.”
- **Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P1] Named destinations and shared state are not trustworthy

- **Why it matters:** Check-in routine progress and products can contradict Routine and Shelf. “Skin profile & ingredients” and “Reminder preferences” both open the first product.
- **Fix:** Derive routine progress, populate products from the shelf, and build dedicated settings destinations or remove/label unavailable rows.
- **Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape`

### [P1] Bottom sheets lack a universal escape and draft safety

- **Why it matters:** Invisible backdrops and decorative handles make users feel trapped and allow accidental draft loss.
- **Fix:** Standardize visible Close/Cancel controls, Escape and swipe-down support, focus return, and dirty-form confirmation.
- **Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

### [P2] The polished visual system runs at an inaccessible scale

- **Why it matters:** 8–10px labels and several 16–43px targets hurt low-vision, motor-impaired, and distracted mobile users.
- **Fix:** Set a 12px tertiary-text floor, 14–16px reading copy, and 44×44px minimum touch targets; test at 200% zoom and 320px width.
- **Suggested command:** `$impeccable audit`

## Persona Red Flags

### Jordan — first-timer adding a product

The Add tab and scan/manual choice are clear. Jordan loses confidence at “BARCODE MICROSERVICE,” sees a camera-like scanner that behaves as prefilled barcode entry, and cannot find a visible close or help control.

### Casey — distracted one-handed mobile user checking in

The Save action and chips are thumb-friendly, but Casey must remember and re-enter routine progress, reselect from a fixed product list, and can lose the sheet state by tapping outside.

### Sam — accessibility-dependent routine user

Sam benefits from labelled navigation, ARIA states, live regions, and focus outlines, but encounters 8–10px text, sub-44px targets, gesture-led controls, and invisible backdrop exits without Escape handling.

## Minor Observations

- `Suncare` exists as a category but has no Shelf filter.
- Shelf health is prominent but its formula and improvement path are opaque.
- Settings chevrons promise navigation, but outcomes range from real sheets to unrelated product details and toasts.
- Gender collection during onboarding has unclear product value.
- Ingredient analysis uses a fixed demo list rather than the selected product’s ingredients.
- The desktop brand rail makes the product read partly as a portfolio mockup instead of a production app.

## Questions to Consider

- Is ShelfCare a trusted care companion or a microservice showcase?
- If users cannot explain why shelf health is 85%, should it be the dominant metric?
- Why can a diary entry disagree with the Routine screen?
- What is the one universal dismissal rule for every sheet?
- Which Settings destinations are real commitments, and which should be removed until they work?
